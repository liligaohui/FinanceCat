# What is the CFA?

The **Chartered Financial Analyst (CFA)** designation is one of the most respected credentials in the investment management industry. Awarded by the CFA Institute, it is recognised globally by employers in asset management, investment banking, research, and risk management.

## The three levels

| Level | Focus | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Level 1 | Knowledge & comprehension of tools and concepts | 180 multiple choice questions |
| Level 2 | Application of tools in valuation contexts | Item sets (vignette-based MCQ) |
| Level 3 | Portfolio management and wealth planning | Item sets + constructed response |

Each level is offered twice a year (February/August for L1, May/November for L2 & L3).

## What this guide covers

This site covers **Level 1** across all ten topic areas, plus selected deeper dives for Levels 2 and 3. Each chapter follows the same structure:

- **Key concepts** — the core ideas you must understand
- **Formulas** — every formula you need, explained in plain English
- **Common exam traps** — where candidates typically lose marks
- **Practice questions** — to test your understanding

## Study tips

- The CFA Institute recommends **300+ hours** of study per level. Most candidates spread this over 4–6 months.
- Do **practice questions from day one**, not just at the end. Reading alone is not enough.
- Ethics is tested at every level and is often the deciding factor for borderline candidates. Take it seriously.
- Use the **Learning Outcome Statements (LOS)** from the CFA curriculum as your checklist — if you can do everything on the LOS, you're ready.
