# Ethics & Professional Standards

Ethics is the **most important topic** in the CFA exam — not just because it carries significant weight, but because it is the tiebreaker. If two candidates score the same on every other topic, the one who scores higher in Ethics passes.

## The Code of Ethics

CFA members and candidates must act with integrity, competence, diligence, and respect. The six components of the Code:

1. Act with integrity, competence, diligence, and respect
2. Place client interests above your own
3. Use reasonable care and independent professional judgement
4. Practice and encourage others to practise ethically
5. Promote the integrity of and uphold the rules governing capital markets
6. Maintain and improve professional competence

## Standard I — Professionalism

**I(A) Knowledge of the Law** — Follow the stricter of local law or the CFA Standards. If they conflict, follow the stricter one. If local law requires something unethical, dissociate.

**I(B) Independence and Objectivity** — Do not accept gifts that could compromise your objectivity. Modest gifts are acceptable; anything that could influence your work is not.

**I(C) Misrepresentation** — Never misrepresent your qualifications, performance, or the nature of your services. Plagiarism falls here too.

**I(D) Misconduct** — Do not engage in dishonesty, fraud, or any act that reflects adversely on your professional reputation.

## Common exam traps

- **Materiality** — information doesn't have to be public to be non-material. Even public information can be material.
- **Mosaic theory** — combining non-material non-public information with public information to reach a conclusion is fine. Acting on material non-public information is not.
- **Soft dollars** — using client brokerage to pay for research is acceptable only if it directly benefits clients.
- **Referral fees** — must be disclosed to clients and employers before entering the arrangement.

## Key formula

There are no formulas in Ethics, but remember this decision tree:

> Is it legal? → Does it comply with CFA Standards? → Is it ethical?

All three must be yes.
