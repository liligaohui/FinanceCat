# What is Quantitative Finance?

Quantitative finance is the application of **mathematical and statistical methods** to financial markets, instruments, and risk. It sits at the intersection of finance, mathematics, statistics, and computer science.

## What quants actually do

| Role | What they build |
|---|---|
| Derivatives pricing | Models to price options, swaps, structured products |
| Risk management | VaR models, stress tests, credit risk models |
| Quantitative research | Factor models, alpha signals, backtests |
| Algorithmic trading | Execution algorithms, market microstructure models |
| Portfolio construction | Optimisation, factor exposure management |

## The core toolkit

To work in quantitative finance you need to be comfortable with:

- **Probability & Statistics** — the language of uncertainty
- **Linear algebra** — the language of portfolios and factor models
- **Calculus** — the language of continuous-time models and optimisation
- **Stochastic calculus** — the language of derivatives pricing
- **Programming** — Python and R are the most common tools

## How this guide is structured

This guide builds the mathematical foundations before moving into applications:

1. **Foundations** — probability, linear algebra, calculus
2. **Intermediate** — stochastic processes, options pricing, risk models, time series
3. **Advanced** — machine learning applications

Each chapter includes the theory, worked examples, and connections to real financial applications. You do not need to be a mathematician — but you do need to be comfortable with abstraction and precise thinking.

## Is this for me?

If you are studying for the CFA or FRM, the Foundations section is directly relevant — especially probability and statistics. If you want to move into a quantitative role, you will need everything here plus strong programming skills.
